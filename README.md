# Tennis Match Simulation
In the Spring of 2023, I took a class at Columbia called Applied Probability and Stochastic Processes with Dr. Sumit Mukherjee. For our final project, Professor Mukherjee let us choose from a series of articles to summarize and present to the class. I chose one entitled "Probability and Tennis" published in *Data Genetics* which calculated a player's probability of winning a match based on the probability that they would win any individual point.

As the article notes, "as a match of tennis contains many point battles, and tennis has some quirky score advancing rules; these concentrate the advantage that a more skillful player has to win the match." In my initial project, I was interested to see if I could build a Monte Carlo simulation based on real-life matches that supported the article's conclusions of a concentrated advantage. In the years since that presentation, I have tried to enhance that simulation.

This is still a work in progress, but Parts 1 \& 2 are illustrative of the work that I am capable of doing and hope to continue.
